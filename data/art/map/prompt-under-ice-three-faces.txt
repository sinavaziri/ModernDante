The first image is a detail of a painting in the style of a Gustave Doré steel engraving glazed with oil color, illustrating the last canto of Dante's Inferno, a classic of world literature: Lucifer, a winged giant with three faces, frozen up to his chest in the lake of ice at the center of the earth, with a sinner in each of his three mouths.

The second image is Gustave Doré's own Lucifer from his engraving of this scene, colored. It is the model for how the giant should be drawn and for the mood.

Repaint the giant's head, his hands and the three sinners in the first image so that they look like Doré drew them, and keep everything else exactly as it is.

The head: one head with three faces under one mass of curly hair, as now, but drawn as Doré draws his: a brooding, heavy-browed, bearded face, sullen and grieving rather than snarling, with deep shadow under the brow, lit coldly from the ice below. The middle face is dull red and looks straight out at the viewer. The face at the viewer's left is pale yellowish, the face at the viewer's right is black; each is turned in profile over its shoulder and half lost in shadow and hair. Tears run from the eyes. Make the horns smaller and plainer, like the short horns in the second image; remove the two huge curved horns and paint dark sky and wing where they were.

The hands: the giant's two forearms stay raised in front of his chest. His hands are large, bony and human, as in the second image, without long claws; they hold the legs of the middle sinner under his chin.

The three sinners need the most work: at present they are stretched into long tubes with missing limbs. Draw each as a small, complete, naturally proportioned naked man in Doré's manner, like the damned in his other Inferno plates: lean, pale, softly modelled with fine hatching, limp. Each is small beside the giant: a whole sinner is about as long as one of the giant's faces is tall. Nothing is stretched or elongated, and each body has a normal neck, two arms, a waist, two legs.
- Middle mouth: the sinner went in head-first. Only his hips and two legs hang out below the lips, bent at the knee, short and limp.
- Each side mouth: the sinner is bitten at the thighs, so his legs are inside the mouth. His torso, two arms and head hang straight down from the lips, head lowest, arms dangling past the head. The hanging part is short, no longer than the giant's face.
No gore and no wounds.

Do not change anything else: the two great purple bat wings, the giant's shoulders, upper arms and chest with their grey shaggy hide, the dark sky, the icicles, the flat frozen lake with the small posts in it, and the blocks of ice across the bottom all stay exactly where they are, at the same size, with the same colors and light. The head stays the same size and in the same place.

Fine engraved hatching and cross-hatching in long parallel lines, as in the second image, with the linework visible under thin glazes of color. No text, no letters, no border, no frame, no signature. Do not crop and do not zoom.

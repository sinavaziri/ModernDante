Two images are given. The FIRST image is a simple flat diagram: a cross-section through solid rock, showing the hidden way out of Hell at the end of Dante's Inferno, a classic of world literature. At the bottom is a dark cavern at the center of the earth. From it a narrow channel winds upward through the rock, with a small stream running down it, to a round opening at the top, where it comes out on a sea shore under a starry sky before dawn. The SECOND image is a reference sheet of four labelled illustrations by Gustave Doré (1868) for the same poem, shown in color.

Repaint the FIRST image as a finished work of art, so that each part follows Doré's own picture of that place: his figures, poses, rock, lighting and color, as listed below. The second image is only a reference; do not reproduce the sheet, its grid or its labels.

Keep the layout of the first image exactly: the same tall format, the cavern across the bottom, the single winding channel in the same place climbing through the middle, and the shore, sea and sky across the top. Everything between the channel and the edges of the picture is solid, dark, layered rock seen in section, as if the earth were cut open.

Throughout, as in Doré: the two travellers appear as a pair of small figures, one in a long red robe and red cap (Dante), one in a blue robe with a laurel wreath (Virgil). They appear three times: in the cavern, on the path, and on the shore. Figures are small, as on a map.

The three parts, from the BOTTOM of the picture to the TOP:
1. The cavern (Plate 73): a rough natural cave with an uneven floor and very little light. Through its floor of pale blue ice rise the two enormous shaggy legs of a giant, from the thighs up, pointing straight upward with the feet toward the roof; the rest of him is hidden below the ice. The red and blue pair stand at the left on a ledge of rock, looking up at the legs.
2. The hidden path (Plate 74): a steep, narrow, winding passage through the rock, dark, with a thin stream of clear water trickling down it over the stones. Half way up, the red and blue pair climb, the blue one leading, the red one behind.
3. The way out (Plates 75 and 77): the passage ends in a round opening in the ground. Above it, the open air: a sandy shore with reeds, a calm sea to the horizon, and a deep blue sky full of stars with one very bright star, turning rose and gold at the horizon. The red and blue pair stand on the shore beside the opening, looking up at the stars.

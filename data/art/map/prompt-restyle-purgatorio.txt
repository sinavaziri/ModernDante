The first image is a band from a tall painting, in the style of a Gustave Doré steel engraving glazed with oil color, of the mountain of Purgatory from Dante's Purgatorio, a classic of world literature: a terraced mountain rising from the sea at dawn, with a small scene on each terrace.

The second image is a detail from the same series, drawn as Gustave Doré draws. Use it only as the model for linework and for how figures are drawn. Do not take its darkness or its colors.

Give the first image a light restoration, not a new picture. Keep every scene, figure, angel, tree, rock, ledge, cloud and wave exactly where it is, at the same size and in the same pose, with the same edges to the image. Keep the colors and the light exactly as they are: the soft dawn sky, the warm pale rock, the greens. Do not darken the picture and do not mute its colors. Change only two things, gently:
- Linework: bring out fine engraved hatching and cross-hatching that follows the forms, on rock, figures, drapery, trees and sky, visible under the color.
- Figures: draw them a little more firmly, as Doré does: naturalistic anatomy, sculptural drapery, grave faces; nothing smooth or doll-like. The two small robed travellers who appear on each terrace, one in a red robe and cap and one in a blue-grey robe, stay exactly as they are, clear and separate.

No text, no letters, no border, no frame, no signature. Do not crop and do not zoom.
